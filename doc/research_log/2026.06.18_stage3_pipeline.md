# 2026.06.18 — Stage 3: pipeline comparison M0/M1/M3 (RQ2)

## Goal

Quantify RQ2: the downstream energy-label cost of replacing registry (GT)
attributes with vision-extracted attributes, via the M3 − M1 gap, through the
same TABULA + LightGBM pipeline.

Plan: `doc_processed/plan/2026.06.18_stage3_pipeline.md`.

## Setup

- LightGBM trained once on full dev (8,068, GT S_full features, fixed HP,
  class_weight=None — identical to Stage 2 M1). Applied to hold-out.
- Evaluated on hold-out, common building set across all routes: **n=2,016**
  (VLM dropped 2 unparsed).
- Routes share the downstream; differ only in type/year/floor source:
  M0 = dev majority (C); M1 = GT; M3-{DINOv2,ResNet50,VLMv3} = vision preds.
  M3 U-values looked up from PREDICTED type×period; city is GT metadata.
- Swin-T, M2 (end-to-end), LOCO deferred.
- Code: `src/stage3/{features,routes,run_stage3}.py`.

## Error propagation — best M3 (DINOv2), of 2,016

- misclassified: 1,428 (71%)

- attrs correct but LightGBM still wrong (downstream): **1,175 (82% of errors)**

## Interpretation (RQ2)

1. **The cost depends entirely on the metric.** On macro-F1 the M3−M1 gap is tiny
   (−0.02 to −0.035): vision attributes look almost as cheap as GT. But on ordinal
   κ the gap is large (−0.14 to −0.19): M1's κ 0.233 collapses to 0.04–0.09. The
   macro-F1 smallness is an artefact of the low ceiling (both M1 and M3 fail the
   rare classes equally); κ exposes the real damage — vision year/period errors
   scramble the ordinal A–G ranking. **Report both; κ is the honest headline.**

2. **Paradigm ranking matches Stage 1**: M3-DINOv2 ≈ M3-ResNet50 > M3-VLMv3.
   Deployment recommendation when registries are missing: DINOv2 frozen (or
   ResNet-50). VLM zero-shot is the weakest but non-trivial.

3. **The bottleneck is the downstream task, not vision** (error propagation):
   82% of M3-DINOv2's errors occur on buildings whose type AND period were
   predicted correctly — the energy label simply isn't determined by these
   attributes (consistent with Stage 2's low GT ceiling and Hettinga 2023, who
   needed floor-area/envelope/socioeconomic features to reach 71%). Only 18% of
   errors trace to vision misprediction. → Investing in better vision models has
   limited downstream payoff here; the limiting factor is feature coverage.

4. **RQ2 answer, framed**: replacing registry with vision attributes costs little
   in macro-F1 but substantially in ordinal agreement (κ −0.15 for the best M3),
   and that cost is bounded below by an already-low, feature-limited ceiling. The
   honest contribution is the decomposed quantification (M3−M1 on both metrics)
   plus the error-propagation attribution showing the ceiling — not vision — is
   the binding constraint.
