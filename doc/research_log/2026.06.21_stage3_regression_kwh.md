# 2026.06.21 — Regression-to-kWh variant (does regressing real energy help?)

## Motivation

User question: would regressing continuous energy use (kWh/m2) and then binning
to A-G beat direct A-G classification? Earlier we thought no continuous target
existed (EnergieIndex was empty). Re-inspection of the raw EP-Online CSV found
the real NTA8800 continuous columns ARE present: `PrimaireFossieleEnergie`
(primary fossil energy, kWh/m2.yr) — the very quantity the A-G label is cut from.

## Data

- `src/stage2/extract_kwh.py`: parse raw EP CSV (header at row 2), keep residential
  (Gebouwklasse=W), PF in [0,1000], most recent per pand_id →
  `data/processed/ep_kwh.parquet` (1.55M pand_id; median 176 kWh/m2.yr).
- Coverage on our buildings: dev 99.9% (8,051/8,068), holdout 99.9%.

## Boundaries (official NTA8800 residential, cross-validated)

A≤160 / B≤190 / C≤250 / D≤290 / E≤335 / F≤380 / G>380 kWh/m2.yr (A merges
A+..A++++). Source: Regeling energieprestatie gebouwen (wetten.overheid.nl
BWBR0020921, Bijlage I) + RVO; cross-checked against per-class PF percentiles in
our own EP data (p1/p99 match the official bounds).

## Method

LightGBM regressor (objective=L1) on dev GT S_full features → PF kWh. Evaluated on
the same holdout common set (n≈2,016): regression MAE/R2, plus bin-to-A-G
macro-F1/kappa vs the direct-classification Stage 3 numbers. M1-reg uses GT
features; M3-{model}-reg use vision-predicted features. `src/stage3/regression_kwh.py`.

## Results (hold-out)

| Route | kWh MAE | kWh R² | mF1 reg→bin | mF1 cls | κ reg→bin | κ cls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| M1-reg (GT) | 54.8 | 0.140 | 0.165 | **0.172** | 0.217 | **0.233** |
| M3-DINOv2-reg | 59.2 | 0.047 | 0.127 | **0.150** | 0.110 | 0.083 |
| M3-ResNet50-reg | 60.2 | 0.030 | 0.130 | **0.149** | 0.119 | 0.091 |
| M3-VLMv3-reg | 64.7 | −0.106 | 0.118 | **0.137** | 0.043 | 0.041 |

## Conclusion

1. **Regression-to-kWh does NOT beat direct classification.** Binned macro-F1 is
   equal-or-lower on every route (M1 0.165 vs 0.172). Binning a noisy continuous
   prediction back into 7 ordinal classes loses information rather than adding it.

2. **The kWh regression itself is weak even with GT features**: M1-reg R² = 0.14,
   MAE 55 kWh/m2 (≈30% of the 176 median). Vision features collapse to R² 0.03 to
   −0.11. This is direct, physical-unit evidence (not just a classification score)
   that type/year/floor explain little of the actual primary fossil energy use —
   consistent with the low classification ceiling and the 82%-downstream error
   propagation. Matches Hettinga (2023): the predictive power lives in floor-area /
   envelope / socioeconomic features, which are invisible to both registry-3-attrs
   and SVI.

3. **Thesis value**: pre-empts the "why not regress real energy?" reviewer
   question with data — we did, on the official NTA8800 quantity, and it is not
   better; the binding constraint is feature coverage, not the classification head.
