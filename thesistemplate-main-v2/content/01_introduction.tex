\section{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:background_motivation}

The building sector accounts for approximately 40\% of final energy consumption in the European Union and generates approximately 36\% of its energy-related greenhouse gas emissions.
The 2024 recast of the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD) further established the policy objective of transforming the existing building stock into a zero-emission building stock by 2050 \parencite{epbd2024}.
However, the actual coverage of Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) data remains incomplete.
The Netherlands introduced its EPC scheme in 2008, but by the end of 2019, only approximately 3.8 million residential buildings had a valid, registered EPC, representing around 48\% of the residential building stock \parencite{caepbd2020}.
Large-scale model-based estimation can therefore complement formal energy certification for building-stock analysis and preliminary retrofit prioritisation, although its use should be clearly distinguished from legally issued EPC certification.

\Acf{UBEM} provides a bottom-up framework for analysing the operational energy use of tens to thousands of buildings at neighbourhood or city scale \parencite{reinhart2016}.
Because collecting detailed information on the geometry, construction, systems, and use of individual buildings is prohibitively costly, many \ac{UBEM} workflows employ an archetype-based approach.
Buildings with similar characteristics are grouped into categories, and each building inherits the thermal and system parameters associated with its archetype.
TABULA is a representative European building-typology framework that defines residential typologies for 20 European countries according to building size, construction period, and other country-specific parameters \parencite{loga2016}.
In the TABULA-NL configuration adopted in this study, building type and the construction period derived from construction year jointly determine the archetype cell, while floor count serves as an additional feature in the subsequent energy classification.
Consequently, the large-scale application of archetype-based \ac{UBEM} requires not only building geometry but also semantic attributes that are frequently missing, including building type, construction year, and floor count.

Previous research has shown that archetype lookup provides a consistent representation of the building stock but cannot capture all building-specific variation.
In a large-scale evaluation using approximately 2.57 million Dutch residential EPC records, TABULA rule-based assignment achieved an accuracy of 26\%, below the majority-class baseline of 33\%, whereas a random forest incorporating additional building-level and neighbourhood-level open-data features achieved 71\% accuracy \parencite{hettinga2023}.
Because the TABULA and machine-learning results were calculated using different evaluation subsets and did not cover identical output ranges, these values indicate the additional information provided by open-data features rather than constituting a fully equivalent model benchmark.
Nevertheless, they show that assigning energy classes solely through typology rules has limited ability to capture variation at the individual-building level.

Recent advances in remote sensing and \acf{CV} offer another means of obtaining missing building attributes.
A workflow combining aerial imagery, digital-surface-model-derived geometry, morphometric construction-type classification, and census-based construction-period data has been incorporated into urban heat-demand modelling \parencite{wurm2021}.
Building-age prediction from spatial attributes has also been evaluated in terms of its effect on residential heat-demand estimates \parencite{garbasevschi2021}.
Direct image-to-energy-class classification has been investigated in the United Kingdom using street-view, aerial, footprint, and land-surface-temperature data, and in Denmark using street-view and aerial imagery \parencite{mayer2023uk,mayer2023dk}.
OpenFACADES, meanwhile, demonstrated the feasibility of large-scale, multi-attribute building-profile enrichment using \acf{SVI} and \acfp{VLM} \parencite{liang2025}.

However, although previous studies have linked inferred building attributes to energy models or archetype assignment, they generally do not provide a controlled, building-paired evaluation in which reference and \ac{SVI}-derived attributes pass through the same TABULA-grounded workflow while the evaluated buildings, data partitions, and downstream classifier are held constant \parencite{garbasevschi2021,wurm2021,dabrock2025}.
Specifically, it remains unclear how much agreement with reference archetype assignments is retained when building type, construction year, and floor count inferred from \ac{SVI} replace reference attributes; which inferred attributes remain informative for archetype assignment and downstream energy-class classification; and what downstream performance costs and geographic transfer boundaries arise from this substitution.
This gap motivates the present study and leads to the three research objectives presented in the following section.

\section{Research Objectives}
\label{sec:research_objectives}

Large-scale applications of archetype-based \ac{UBEM} require the metadata needed to assign individual buildings to appropriate archetypes.
However, open geospatial datasets in many settings primarily describe building geometry, while semantic attributes such as building type, construction year, and floor count are incomplete or unavailable.
The Netherlands provides a data-rich setting in which administrative attributes, reconstructed geometry, and registered energy classes can be linked for the same buildings, enabling controlled evaluation of \ac{SVI}-derived substitutes.
In TABULA-NL, building type and construction period, derived here from construction year, determine the archetype cell; floor count is retained as an additional predictor in the downstream energy-class classification task.

The overall objective of this thesis is to assess how replacing reference attributes obtained from administrative records and reconstructed 3D building data with attributes inferred from \ac{SVI} affects a TABULA-based building-stock enrichment workflow, and to identify the evaluated conditions under which performance is retained.
This overall objective is addressed through three specific research objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Evaluate the predictive performance of \ac{SVI}-based extraction of building type, construction year, and floor count.}
	      This objective compares a fully fine-tuned \acf{CNN}, a frozen self-supervised backbone with supervised prediction heads, and \ac{VLM} inference without parameter updating on the same three building attributes.
	\item \textbf{Determine whether \ac{SVI}-derived attributes reproduce reference TABULA-NL archetype assignments and quantify the physically weighted differences associated with assignment errors.}
	      This objective compares reference and \ac{SVI}-derived archetype cells and expresses their differences through the resulting archetype-derived transmission heat-transfer coefficient.
	      This measure compares archetype assignments under common geometric and thermal assumptions rather than validating them against measured heat loss.
	\item \textbf{Quantify the downstream performance cost and geographic transfer of replacing reference attributes with \ac{SVI}-derived attributes in energy-class classification.}
	      Under a controlled protocol that holds the buildings, data splits, and downstream classifier constant, this objective measures the change in classification performance, identifies the contribution of individual attributes, and evaluates performance in Amsterdam as a single city excluded from training.
\end{enumerate}

To support these objectives, this thesis constructs a three-layer, TABULA-grounded semi-synthetic building dataset in which the provenance of each value is retained.
The reference layer contains administrative observations, reconstructed geometry, and EP-Online reference energy classes; the vision-inference layer contains \ac{SVI}-derived attributes; and the synthetic archetype layer contains the TABULA cells and parameters deterministically assigned from the reference or inferred attributes.
A downstream energy-class classification case study is then used to examine how replacing the attribute source affects an energy-related classification task.
