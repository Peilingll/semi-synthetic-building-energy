\section*{Abstract}

Large-scale archetype-based urban building energy modelling relies on semantic building metadata that are often incomplete or missing from open geospatial datasets. This study evaluates whether building type, construction year, and floor count inferred from street view imagery (SVI) can replace reference attributes from administrative records and reconstructed 3D data in a TABULA-NL building stock enrichment workflow. A traceable semi-synthetic dataset comprises 47,150 images linked to 10,086 residential buildings in four Dutch cities. Three models were evaluated: a ResNet-50 fine-tuned across all layers, a frozen DINOv2 backbone with supervised prediction heads, and zero-shot InternVL3-2B. Evaluation spanned attribute extraction, TABULA-NL archetype assignment, physically weighted discrepancies from reference archetypes, and downstream classification of energy classes under controlled attribute substitution.

DINOv2 performed best on six of seven attribute metrics, including a mean absolute error of 9.45 years for construction year. Its joint-cell accuracy exceeded both the uniform random expectation and the majority-cell baseline. Holding the reconstructed building areas and window-to-wall ratios constant, its archetype assignments overestimated stock-level transmission heat loss by approximately 10\% relative to reference assignments. Substituting DINOv2 predictions for reference attributes had little effect on binary macro-F1. However, both the reference route and the route based on inferred attributes had low recall for classes D--G. Direct image classification achieved the highest binary macro-F1. When Amsterdam was excluded from model development, construction-year error increased, and trained downstream routes approached the uniform random baseline in that city.

The findings support the conditional use of attributes derived from SVI when reference metadata are unavailable. Suitability depends on the attribute, downstream task, sample composition, and geographic relationship between training and application data.
