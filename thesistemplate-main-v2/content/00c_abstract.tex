\section*{Abstract}

Archetype-based urban building energy modelling requires building type, construction year, and floor count, yet these semantic attributes are often unavailable.
This thesis evaluates whether attributes inferred from street-view imagery (SVI) can substitute for reference attributes derived from administrative records and reconstructed 3D data in a TABULA-NL building-stock enrichment workflow.
A traceable semi-synthetic dataset was constructed for 10,086 residential buildings and 47,150 images across four Dutch cities.
Three inference configurations---a fully fine-tuned ResNet-50, a frozen DINOv2 backbone with supervised prediction heads, and zero-shot InternVL3-2B---were evaluated for attribute extraction, archetype assignment, archetype-derived transmission heat-transfer differences, and downstream energy-class classification under controlled comparisons.

DINOv2 provided the most consistent performance, achieving a construction-year mean absolute error of 9.45 years, a period accuracy of 0.901, and a joint TABULA-NL cell accuracy of 0.825.
However, the latter exceeded the majority-cell baseline by only 3.5 percentage points, reflecting sample concentration.
DINOv2-derived archetypes produced a stock-level transmission heat-loss bias of $+10.0$\% relative to reference assignments.
Replacing reference attributes with DINOv2 predictions changed binary macro-F1 only from 0.492 to 0.490, but the routes showed weak recall for buildings in classes D--G.
Direct DINOv2 image classification reached the highest binary macro-F1 of 0.597, while seven-class performance remained low across all routes.
When Amsterdam was excluded from model development, construction-year error increased and trained routes approached random-guessing performance.

The results show that SVI-derived attributes can supplement missing metadata but are not equivalent replacements.
Their utility depends on the attribute, target task, sample composition, and geographic relationship between training and application data.
