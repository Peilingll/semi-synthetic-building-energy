\section*{Abstract}

Large-scale archetype-based urban building energy modelling depends on semantic building metadata that are incomplete or unavailable in many open geospatial datasets.
This thesis assesses whether building type, construction year, and floor count inferred from street-view imagery (SVI) can substitute for reference attributes obtained from administrative records and reconstructed 3D data within a TABULA-NL building-stock enrichment workflow.
A traceable semi-synthetic dataset links 47,150 images to 10,086 residential buildings across four Dutch cities.
Three configurations were evaluated: a fully fine-tuned ResNet-50, a frozen DINOv2 backbone with supervised prediction heads, and zero-shot InternVL3-2B.
The evaluation covered attribute extraction, TABULA-NL archetype assignment, physically weighted differences between assigned archetypes, and downstream energy-class classification under controlled attribute substitution.

DINOv2 achieved the best results in six of seven attribute metrics, including a mean absolute error of 9.45 years for construction year.
Its joint-cell accuracy exceeded both the uniform random expectation and the majority-cell baseline.
With the reconstructed building areas and window-to-wall ratio held constant, DINOv2 assignments overestimated stock-level transmission heat loss by approximately 10\% relative to reference archetype assignments.
Replacing reference attributes with DINOv2 predictions had little effect on binary macro-F1, but both the reference and attribute-mediated routes showed low recall for classes D to G.
Direct image classification with DINOv2 achieved the highest binary macro-F1.
Excluding Amsterdam from model development increased construction-year error, and trained downstream routes approached the uniform random expectation in Amsterdam.

The findings support only conditional use of SVI-derived attributes when reference metadata are unavailable.
Their suitability depends on the attribute, downstream task, sample composition, and geographic relationship between training and application data.
