\section{Overview}
\label{sec:method_overview}

\subsection{Pipeline Architecture}
\label{sec:pipeline_architecture}

This study uses a sequential pipeline to evaluate how substituting reference building attributes with attributes inferred from \ac{SVI} affects TABULA-NL archetype assignment, derived thermal parameters, and downstream energy class classification.
\Cref{fig:pipeline_overview} presents the four processing stages and shows how they correspond to the three research objectives.

The first stage pairs street-view images with the target buildings and retains information on image source, view quality, and pairing status.
The second stage uses the eligible images to infer building type, construction year, and floor count.
Predicted values and invalid responses are recorded separately.
The third stage converts the inferred building type and construction year into a TABULA-NL archetype cell through a deterministic lookup.
This step provides the associated U-values and preserves their provenance.
The final stage assembles the features used by the downstream energy class classifier and prepares the geometric and thermal inputs required to calculate $H'_{tr}$.

The outputs of this pipeline form the basis of the controlled evaluation described in \cref{ch:experiments}.
The evaluation compares reference attributes with attributes inferred from \ac{SVI} under common data partitions and fixed downstream protocols.
It also examines direct energy class prediction from imagery, geographic transfer, and the physical consistency of the substituted thermal parameters.

\subsection{Semi-Synthetic Dataset: Three-Layer Structure}
\label{sec:three_layer_structure}

This study defines the semi-synthetic dataset as three layers with explicitly separated provenance.
First, the reference layer contains administrative records from BAG and EP-Online, reconstructed geometry from 3DBAG, and the reference energy class adopted in this study.
These fields provide the reference observations required for model training, attribute substitution, and downstream evaluation, but they are not collectively treated as error-free ground truth.
In particular, the reference energy class remains affected by multiple certificates and within-building class disagreement; its quality is audited separately in \cref{sec:building_level_consistency}.

Second, the vision-inference layer stores the building-level predictions of building type, construction year, and floor count produced by the three vision paradigms, together with their model provenance.
Third, the synthetic archetype layer derives the construction period from the estimated year, deterministically maps the pair (type, period) to a TABULA-NL cell, and assigns the cell's four as-built envelope U-values for the wall, roof, floor, and windows.
These U-values are archetype-level average assumptions for the corresponding building stock subgroup; they are not measurements of the thermal performance of individual buildings and do not introduce new building-specific observations.

Here, semi-synthetic refers to the combination of reference observations, image-derived attributes, and deterministically assigned archetype parameters.
The energy class target remains an administrative reference obtained from EP-Online rather than a simulated class; only the archetype parameters assigned through the TABULA cell are synthetic.
This separation makes the provenance of each value traceable and provides the common data foundation for the three research objectives.

In the controlled substitution comparison, M1 and M3 use the same eligible buildings, geographic split, feature positions, and fixed model for each downstream task.
M1 uses the reference type, year, and floor attributes and obtains the TABULA U-values from the corresponding reference (type, period) cell.
M3 replaces these inputs with the type, year, and floor attributes inferred from \ac{SVI} and retrieves the U-values from the resulting predicted cell.
Within this protocol, the difference between M3 and M1 therefore measures the consequence of substituting the attribute source rather than the effect of retraining the downstream model.
M2 predicts energy class directly from imagery and does not pass through this three-layer substitution chain; it is consequently treated as a parallel end-to-end comparator.

\section{Dataset Construction}
\label{sec:dataset_construction}

\subsection{Sources}
\label{sec:data_sources}

Building-level linkage among BAG, EP-Online, and Mapillary forms the core sample.
3DBAG provides the reconstructed geometry used in subsequent stages \parencite{peters2022,dukai2021}, while TABULA-NL provides a deterministic archetype lookup based on building type and construction period.

Mapillary was selected instead of a commercial street-view service because its open licensing supports a reproducible research workflow \parencite{mapillary}.
However, its image coverage is determined by contributor activity and does not constitute a random sample of the building stock.
The resulting dataset may therefore be biased towards image-dense urban centres and particular residential types.
Representative cropped street-view images are shown in \cref{fig:svi_examples}; the coverage bias is quantified through the sample distributions in \cref{sec:dataset_statistics}, and its implications for the scope of the study are discussed in \cref{sec:limitations}.

The downstream targets are constructed directly from the registered EP-Online energy class, with ratings from A+ through A++++ merged into class A.
The primary task is an independently trained binary classifier that groups energy classes into A to C and D to G.
The division corresponds to the upper boundary of class C at 250\,kWh/(m$^2\cdot$year).
Seven-class A to G prediction is retained as a diagnostic task for examining exact-class classification.
Both tasks use the registered energy class; continuous energy regression is an auxiliary analysis and does not define the primary targets.

\subsection{SVI Acquisition, Data Integration, and Filtering Rules}
\label{sec:svi_acquisition_filtering}

\textbf{SVI acquisition and image-to-building matching.}
Mapillary street-view imagery was acquired and processed using an adapted implementation of the OpenFACADES framework \parencite{liang2025}, as shown in \cref{fig:svi_workflow}.
OpenFACADES integrates building footprints from OpenStreetMap and Overture Maps before retrieving candidate street-view images.
In the present workflow, footprint acquisition and image processing were separated, and a BAG-based residential prefilter was introduced to restrict image processing to the target residential building stock.

The residential prefilter used a centroid-in-polygon operation.
OpenFACADES footprints and BAG building polygons were projected to the Dutch national coordinate reference system (EPSG:28992), and the centroid of each OpenFACADES footprint was calculated.
A footprint proceeded to image processing only when its centroid lay within a BAG polygon registered for residential use.
This preliminary operation selected the footprints for image processing but did not establish the final match between the \ac{SVI} and reference datasets.

For the retained footprints, OpenFACADES retrieved metadata for Mapillary 360\textdegree{} panoramic images within the study areas.
Isovist analysis with a 30\,m search radius established candidate panorama-to-building pairs based on unobstructed lines of sight between the camera and the target footprint.
Candidate panoramas satisfying the \acf{AoV} criterion were downloaded at a resolution of 2,048 pixels.
GroundingDINO was then used to detect the target building, after which perspective reprojection and cropping produced an individual-building image.
The resulting crops were evaluated using the image-quality criteria reported in \cref{tab:data_criteria}.

Each cropped image initially retained an OpenFACADES building identifier, whereas the reference attributes and energy classes were organised using the BAG building identifier.
The centroid-in-polygon operation was therefore applied again after image processing to match each image to a BAG building.
Images without a valid BAG match were excluded.

Image counts were limited at two levels.
During OpenFACADES candidate selection, no more than three panoramas with the largest \ac{AoV} were retained for each OpenFACADES footprint.
Because multiple OpenFACADES footprints could correspond to the same BAG building, more than three cropped images could remain for a single building after matching.
The images for each BAG building were therefore ranked by decreasing \ac{AoV} and, when \ac{AoV} values were equal, by increasing camera-to-building distance.
No more than eight images were retained per building.

\textbf{Building-level reference data and final integration.}
The building-level reference data and the curated \ac{SVI} dataset were prepared separately.
EP-Online records were restricted to existing residential buildings with a non-missing energy class, and the certificate with the latest registration date was retained for each BAG building.
Eligible reference records also required a construction year between 1800 and 2025, a building type mappable to one of the four residential size classes, and a 3DBAG-derived reference floor count between 1 and 30.
BAG, 3DBAG, and EP-Online records were combined using the BAG building identifier.
Records were excluded when their 3DBAG geometry could not be processed for the required calculations or when a TABULA-NL archetype could not be assigned.
The remaining reference data were then combined with the curated \ac{SVI} dataset.
Uncertainty arising from reducing multiple certificate records to a single building-level target is evaluated in \cref{sec:building_level_consistency}.

Stages 1--6 construct the curated \ac{SVI} dataset, stage 7 defines the eligible building records, and stage 8 produces the final experimental dataset.

Certificates were not explicitly filtered by calculation method.
However, all certificates retained from the EP-Online snapshot dated 1 April 2026 were registered in 2021 or later, during the period in which NTA 8800 \parencite{NTA8800} was in effect.
Registration date alone does not confirm the calculation method used for each certificate.

After image-quality filtering, building matching, and application of the eight-image limit, the curated \ac{SVI} dataset contained 10,104 buildings and 47,238 images.
The final integration excluded 18 buildings and 88 associated images because their 3DBAG geometry could not be processed for the required calculations or because a TABULA-NL archetype could not be assigned.
The final experimental dataset therefore contained 10,086 buildings and 47,150 images.
Mapillary image acquisition dates were not matched to EPC registration dates, creating a possible temporal mismatch between the imagery and the reference energy class; its implications are discussed in \cref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Data Partitioning and Cross-Validation}
\label{sec:data_partitioning}

The final experimental dataset was partitioned at the building level in two stages.
First, a five-fold \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold} partition was generated for the 10,086 buildings.
One fold was reserved as a fixed 20\% holdout set containing 2,018 buildings, while the remaining 8,068 buildings formed the 80\% development set.
A composite stratification label was constructed from the study area, the original EP-Online residential building type, the registered energy class, and the derived TABULA construction period.
The BAG building identifier was used as the grouping variable, ensuring that images of the same building could not appear in different data subsets.

Second, five-fold cross-validation using \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold} was performed within the development set.
Model fitting, hyperparameter selection, and model selection for all trainable configurations were restricted to the development set.
The fixed holdout set was not used for model selection and was accessed only for final evaluation.
All comparison routes used the same partitions, thereby maintaining a common evaluation population across routes.

\section{Vision Inference Strategies and Training Configurations}
\label{sec:vision_inference_strategies}

This study evaluates three predefined open-model configurations representing full-model fine-tuning, supervised prediction heads fitted to a frozen self-supervised backbone, and \ac{VLM} inference without parameter updates.
The configurations differ simultaneously in model architecture, pretraining strategy, parameter-update scope, number of images per building, and multi-image aggregation.
The results therefore compare three complete and reproducible inference configurations; they do not isolate the causal effect of any single training choice through a factorial experiment.
Closed commercial models were excluded because model-version stability, access conditions, and long-term reproducibility could not be controlled.

\textbf{Supervised attribute prediction.}
ResNet-50 and DINOv2 each receive up to eight 224 $\times$ 224-pixel images per building.
Each image is encoded as a feature vector by the corresponding backbone, and the image-level vectors are averaged at the building level.
The averaged representation is passed through a shared 256-dimensional \acf{MLP}, followed by three output heads that predict building type, construction year, and floor count.
The joint attribute-training objective is

\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:attr_loss}
	\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{attr}}
	= \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE,type}}
	+ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE,year}}
	+ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE,floor}} .
\end{equation}

The building type head is optimised using cross-entropy loss with class weights inversely proportional to the class frequencies in the corresponding development training fold.
Construction year and floor count are first standardised using the mean and standard deviation of that training fold and are then evaluated using mean squared error.
The three losses are assigned equal weight, and their weights are not adjusted using holdout data.
The ResNet-50 configuration updates the backbone, shared \ac{MLP}, and all three output heads.
The DINOv2 configuration keeps the backbone frozen and updates only the shared \ac{MLP} and output heads.
ResNet-50 therefore updates approximately 24.0 million parameters in total, comprising 23.5 million backbone parameters and 0.5 million parameters in the shared \ac{MLP} and output heads.
By contrast, the frozen DINOv2 configuration trains approximately 0.2 million parameters, or about 0.2\% of its 85.9 million total parameters.

Both supervised configurations are trained in five folds within the development set.
Early stopping in each fold is based on building type macro-F1 on the corresponding validation partition.
For final holdout evaluation, the fold checkpoint with the highest building type macro-F1 on its development-set validation partition is selected.
The complete training hyperparameters, including the optimiser, learning rates, data augmentation, and early-stopping settings, are reported in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix_b} (\cref{tab:vision_hyperparams}).

\textbf{VLM-based attribute inference.}
InternVL3-2B \parencite{chen2024} does not update its parameters using the study dataset.
Instead, deterministic decoding returns a JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) object containing \texttt{building\_type}, \texttt{construction\_year}, and \texttt{num\_floors}.
The prompt wording and output constraints were developed using a fixed, class-balanced subset of 200 buildings from the development set and were frozen before holdout evaluation.
Accordingly, \emph{zero-shot} in this thesis means that no model parameters were trained or fine-tuned on the study dataset; it does not mean that the complete analysis procedure was developed without access to development labels.

Up to three top-ranked images are used for each building.
Valid image-level responses are aggregated at the building level by taking the mode for building type, the median for construction year, and the rounded median for floor count.
Responses that cannot be parsed or fall outside the permitted ranges are marked as invalid and excluded from aggregation.
The complete wording of the three prompts, decoding settings, and output parsing rules is provided in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix_c}.

\textbf{Direct energy class prediction (M2).}
In addition to the attribute-prediction models used in the M3 route, a direct energy class classification configuration is established for M2.
For ResNet-50 and DINOv2, a single energy class classification head replaces the three attribute heads.
The seven-class A--G task and binary A--C versus D--G task are trained separately using cross-entropy loss with class weights inversely proportional to the class frequencies in the corresponding development training fold.
ResNet-50 is fully fine-tuned for M2.
DINOv2 instead uses pre-extracted and cached building-level mean features, with only the energy class classification network being trained.
Early stopping within each fold and selection of the final fold checkpoint are based on energy class macro-F1 on the corresponding validation partition of the development set.
InternVL3-2B uses separate prompts for direct seven-class and binary prediction.
Each image produces one class response, and the final building-level prediction is determined by majority vote.

Because the two supervised configurations use up to eight images with feature-level aggregation, whereas InternVL3-2B uses up to three images with prediction-level aggregation, the results should be interpreted as comparisons between complete model configurations.
The independent effects of image count and aggregation method are not identified by the experimental design.

\section{TABULA Archetype Matching}
\label{sec:tabula_archetype_matching}

\subsection{TABULA-NL Matrix Structure}
\label{sec:tabula_nl_matrix}

The TABULA matrix for the Netherlands comprises six construction periods and four size classes, forming 24 archetype cells in total (\cref{fig:tabula_matrix}).
Each cell corresponds to a TABULA-NL archetype identifier that is subsequently used to retrieve the associated as-built thermal parameters.
The construction-period boundaries and operational definitions of the size classes are taken from the national TABULA typology reports for the Netherlands, specifically the RVO \emph{Example Dwellings} series \parencite{rvo2011}, rather than from \textcite{loga2016}.
\Citeauthor{loga2016} provide the common methodological framework and cross-country comparison but do not report the country-specific classification thresholds applied here.

\textbf{Size class definitions.}
The TABULA-NL matrix defines four size classes: Single-Family House (SFH), Terraced House (TH), Multi-Family House (MFH), and Apartment Block (AB).
No additional thresholds based on dwelling count or floor count are imposed.
\Cref{tab:type_mapping} presents the deterministic mapping between the retained EP-Online residential building categories and these four size classes.

\subsection{Attribute-to-Archetype Mapping}
\label{sec:attribute_archetype_mapping}

The TABULA-NL archetype cell of each building is jointly determined by its size class and construction period.
Let $s_i$ denote the TABULA size class of building $i$, and let $p(y_i)$ denote the construction period obtained by discretising construction year $y_i$ according to the boundaries shown in \cref{fig:tabula_matrix}.
The archetype cell is then defined as

\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:archetype_cell}
	c_i = \bigl(s_i,\, p(y_i)\bigr).
\end{equation}

In the reference-attribute route, construction year is obtained from BAG, while the EP-Online residential building category is converted deterministically to SFH, TH, MFH, or AB according to \cref{tab:type_mapping}.
In the vision-attribute route, the model predicts these four TABULA size classes directly and does not use the EP-Online category as an intermediate mapping.
The continuous construction year predicted by the model is discretised using the same period boundaries.
Both routes therefore apply the same archetype lookup and differ only in whether the input attributes originate from reference data or vision predictions.

\subsection{U-Value Lookup and As-Built Assumption}
\label{sec:uvalue_lookup}

After archetype-cell assignment, the wall, roof, floor, and window U-values are retrieved from the TABULA-NL lookup table according to the building's size class and construction period.
All four values are expressed in $\mathrm{W/(m^2\,K)}$.
The reference-attribute route performs the lookup using the reference building type and construction year, while the vision-attribute route applies the same procedure using the image-inferred building type and construction year.
\Cref{tab:tabula_uvalues} reports the complete lookup values.
SFH, MFH, and AB share the same values within each construction period, while TH differs only in NL.01; the corresponding rows are therefore combined.
The lookup table was generated programmatically from the TABULA workbook and stored in \texttt{data/processed/tabula\_nl.csv}.
The source of each value is recorded in \texttt{data/processed/\allowbreak tabula\_nl\_provenance.csv}, with the associated audit documented in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix_a}.

Because renovation status is unavailable at the individual-building level, every building is assigned the unrenovated, as-built variant of its TABULA-NL cell.
This assignment is a modelling assumption rather than an observation of the building's current envelope condition.
For thermally renovated buildings, the assigned U-values may exceed the current thermal transmittance and may therefore overestimate transmission heat loss.

TABULA-NL U-values represent archetype-level assumptions for the corresponding building stock subgroup rather than measured thermal parameters for individual buildings \parencite{loga2016}.
Buildings with the same size class and construction period therefore receive the same four U-values.
These values cannot establish the actual envelope construction or renovation status of an individual building.

In addition to serving as inputs to the downstream energy class classifier (\cref{sec:downstream_model}), these U-values are used to calculate the transmission heat transfer coefficient $H'_{tr}$ in the physically weighted archetype-difference evaluation described in \cref{sec:weighted_archetype_metric}.

\section{Downstream Energy Class Classification Model}
\label{sec:downstream_model}

The downstream energy class classification model is implemented using a LightGBM gradient-boosting classifier \parencite{ke2017}.
LightGBM is suitable for low-dimensional tabular data containing both numerical and categorical variables and allows the same model architecture and training configuration to be maintained across attribute sources.
In this study, LightGBM serves as a controlled downstream evaluator for quantifying how replacing reference attributes with \ac{SVI}-derived attributes affects energy class prediction.
The classifier is not presented as a new algorithmic contribution.

The model has eight core input fields: building type, construction year, floor count, wall U-value, roof U-value, floor U-value, window U-value, and city.
Building type and city are treated as categorical features, while the remaining fields are numerical.
The reference-attribute route uses the reference building type, construction year, and floor count and obtains the four U-values from the corresponding TABULA-NL cell.
The vision-attribute route instead uses the three \ac{SVI}-derived attributes and retrieves the U-values from the predicted TABULA-NL cell.
The two routes therefore have the same eight-field feature schema, and city remains unchanged.
The only differences arise from the source of the three upstream attributes and the archetype U-values derived from them.
Apart from the energy class target and the fields required to construct the reference attributes, no other certificate-derived variables are used as predictors because such variables would be unavailable for target buildings without an existing energy certificate.

The seven-class A--G and binary A--C versus D--G energy class tasks use two independently trained LightGBM classifiers with multiclass and binary objectives, respectively.
Both tasks use the same fixed hyperparameter configuration, without route-specific tuning.
The principal settings are 400 boosting iterations, a learning rate of 0.05, 31 leaves, and no class weighting.
The complete configuration is reported in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix_b} (\cref{tab:lightgbm_hyperparams}).

For each task, the final downstream classifier is fitted once on the complete development set using reference features.
The same fitted classifier is then applied separately to the reference-derived and vision-derived holdout features.
This protocol avoids introducing additional variation through downstream-model retraining, so the prediction difference reflects the combined consequences of attribute substitution and propagation through the TABULA lookup.
The development-set evaluation, holdout protocol, and evaluation metrics are described in \cref{sec:strategy_metrics}.
