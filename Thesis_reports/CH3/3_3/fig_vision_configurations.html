Fig. 3.5 — The three vision inference configurations and the scope of training in each. All three lanes take the same per-building street-view crops and end at the same prediction head, so the figure isolates what actually differs between the configurations. The first difference is the extent of the trained region: ResNet-50 updates the backbone, the shared trunk and the heads; DINOv2 leaves the backbone frozen and trains only the trunk and heads; InternVL3-2B updates nothing and is prompted instead. The second is where the several images of one building are combined (blue markers). The two trained configurations aggregate at feature level, averaging the per-image feature vectors before any prediction is made (early fusion), so evidence from different views is pooled before a decision. The VLM aggregates at decision level, producing one answer per image and reducing the answers by mode and median at building level (late fusion), so each view must commit to a decision on its own. The prediction head is shared: a single energy classifier for M2, with the 7-class and binary tasks trained separately, or three attribute heads for M3, with type predicted by classification and year and floor by regression; for the VLM the same substitution is made in the prompt rather than in a head. “Zero-shot” therefore means only that no model parameter is trained on the study data — development labels still enter through the prompt design. Loss terms, weighting and further configuration details are given in the text and in Table 3.3.
